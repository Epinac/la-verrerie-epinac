The Estate

A 19th-century manor comes back to life. Be among the very first to discover it.

At the heart of Épinac, in Southern Burgundy, the estate opens its first two guest rooms on September 21, 2026.

Year · 1841 Grounds · 9,400 m² Location · Épinac, Burgundy
Cul de Bouteille

The deep green of hand-blown glass.

A name inherited from the glassmakers themselves: "cul de bouteille", that dark, thick green of hand-blown bottles, imperfect and alive. Under a soaring ceiling, light glides across a Hungarian-point parquet floor to the original fireplace.

Pierre de Silice

Silica, the base component of glass.

Silica is the base component of glass, the raw material once heated in the glassworks' furnaces before becoming a bottle or flask. This room keeps its pale, mineral tone, under the same soaring ceiling as its neighbour, where morning light falls unobstructed.

The Place

A director's house, set above the furnaces.

At the end of the 18th century, something more precious than vines was found beneath Épinac: coal. For a hundred and fifty years, the village became a factory town.

We bought this house as time had left it. Our project is simple: give it back, little by little, the charm of its past. The first guests will discover an estate in full renaissance, a restoration carried out room by room.

To begin, we are opening two rooms. A table d'hôte will follow, later, as the house comes back to life.
